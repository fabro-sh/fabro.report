# Agent - Triager

## WHAT: Definition

A **Triager** is a member of the Fabro team (currently Bryan + team) who retrieves Debug Bundles for bug investigation **and reads Feedback to understand what self-hosted Fabro users are reporting as bugs, requesting as features, or otherwise saying about the product.** The Triager's single most counterintuitive property — and the one most likely to be eroded by a builder agent working from a "bug-intake service" intuition — is this: **the Triager never interacts with the `fabro.report` service itself, for either intake type.** The service has no Triager-facing surface at all; the team side of the pickup happens entirely out-of-band, for both intake types, though through different external surfaces. The Triager is the **pickup** side of pickup-point; this product exists for their benefit, and precisely because of that, it gives them no surface.

The Triager is **one role with two parallel workflows**, one per intake type, after the 2026-04-23 scope expansion from one intake type (Debug Bundles) to two (Debug Bundles + Feedback):

- **Debug Bundle workflow (primary, unchanged).** The Triager retrieves Debug Bundles directly from S3 using their own AWS credentials, not through any endpoint, API, dashboard, or login on this service. They extract the Report ID from a `fabro.report` URL shared out-of-band (GitHub issue, Slack, email) and use it to locate the Debug Bundle in S3.
- **Feedback workflow (new, parallel).** The Triager reads Feedback posts in a private Discord channel the service pushes Feedback notifications to via webhook. Discord is the **primary surface** for the Feedback workflow. If a Discord post does not carry everything the Triager needs (e.g., a large attachment that was truncated, or the tarball of attachments that accompanied the submission), the Triager may **secondarily** retrieve the full submission from S3 using their own AWS credentials — the same S3-direct retrieval path as the Debug Bundle workflow, but against the Feedback prefix of the bucket and against `feedback.json` / `feedback.txt` / attachment objects rather than a single `bundle.tar.gz`.

Both workflows are strictly out-of-band with respect to `fabro.report` itself. Discord is external. S3 is external. AWS IAM is external. The service's behavior includes posting a Feedback notification to Discord via webhook, but the service has no endpoint a Triager calls, no page a Triager visits, no UI a Triager authenticates into, and no listing, filtering, aggregating, or searching surface of any kind — not for Debug Bundles, not for Feedback, not for anything else. The Triager has no narrow-posture surface to use, and precisely that is the product's commitment.

## WHERE: Ecosystem

- Identity and vocabulary:
  - [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] — the canonical **Triager** definition (a member of the Fabro team who retrieves bundles for bug investigation; **never interacts with the intake service itself**; retrieves bundles directly from S3 using their own AWS credentials; the service has no Triager-facing surface) is the authoritative definition this card expands. The no-service-surface property is vocabulary-level, not implementation-level.
- Counterpart roles:
  - [[Agent - Reporter]] — the self-hosted Fabro user on the other end of the Debug Bundle pickup. The asymmetric-identity model is load-bearing: a Reporter has no identity on the service and interacts with the CLI only; a Triager has full AWS credentials and interacts with S3 only. The two roles sit on opposite sides of the service and neither is ever both.
  - [[Agent - Feedback Submitter]] — the self-hosted Fabro user on the other end of the Feedback pickup (added 2026-04-23 when the product scope expanded from one intake type to two). The Triager reads Feedback in Discord and optionally retrieves the submission from S3; the Feedback Submitter's involvement ends at CLI confirmation. Same no-Triager-surface asymmetry as the Reporter counterpart, landed on a different primary surface (Discord, not a shared capability URL).
- Access boundary:
  - [[Decision - Capability URLs over Auth]] — the capability URL the Reporter shares is a view+delete capability, not a retrieval capability. Triagers do not "unlock" the Debug Bundle through the URL; they locate the Debug Bundle via the Report ID encoded in the URL and retrieve it from S3 with their own AWS identity. The service has no access-control layer for Triagers because Triagers have no request path through the service. AWS IAM is the Triager's access-control layer, and it is orthogonal to this service.
- Scope commitment — this role is the single most load-bearing case of it:
  - [[Principle - Narrow Forever]] — Triager-facing UI (a team dashboard, a list of recent Reports, a filter by Fabro version, a triage UI, any Triager-facing endpoint) is on the **permanently-out-of-scope list**. The service exists for Triagers' benefit and gives them no surface, and that is not a v1 simplification — it is the product's identity. This is the single property most likely to be eroded by a builder agent's assumption that "of course the team needs a dashboard."
- Scope coherence:
  - [[Product Thesis - Pickup Point, Not Triage Surface]] — the Triager is the picker-upper. The thesis says "the service's job ends at 'URL exists,'" which structurally requires that no part of the team's investigation workflow depends on an endpoint of this service. Any card that describes a Triager-facing surface is evidence the thesis has been abandoned.
  - [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]] — `bun.report`-shaped triage tooling (dashboards, per-team scoping, triage UI, symbolication exposed to the team in-browser) is the most common drift vector and the one Triagers are most likely to be imagined as needing. F3 enumerates each of these; this card is the positive statement that Triagers already get what they need (an S3 path) without any of them.
- Strategic why:
  - [[Principle - Strategy Support Effectiveness for Self-Hosted Users]] — the product exists for the Triager's benefit: the Fabro team can help self-hosted users only if the team can retrieve the Debug Bundle, and the Triager is the team member doing the retrieving. Strategy is optimizing for the Triager's access to the bundle, not the Triager's experience on any service. Those two things are deliberately different.
- Audience commitment:
  - [[Decision - Who This Service Is For]] — the audience is self-hosted Fabro users (Reporter side); the Triager side is the Fabro team as it exists today, currently Bryan + team, with no role hierarchy or Triager subtypes. Widening the Triager definition (e.g., to third-party support partners, or to hosted-Fabro team members if that offering ever exists) is an audience change under that Decision, not an implementation change here.
- Referenced by:
  - [[Component - Report]] — the Report carries the ID a Triager copies out of the shared URL to locate the Debug Bundle in S3. The Report's URL is the capability for view + delete on the Metadata Page (Triager's incidental use); the Report ID alone — extracted from the URL — is what the Triager uses against S3 with their own AWS credentials.
  - [[Component - Feedback]] — the Feedback entity the Triager reads via the Discord post (primary surface) and optionally retrieves from S3 using their own AWS credentials (secondary surface). A16 names the entity; this card names the role's two-workflow shape that reads it.
  - [[Component - Report Metadata Page]] — the only surface the Triager ever touches on the service, and only incidentally (to click delete when a Reporter shares the URL and asks for deletion). Any Triager-specific capability on this page is a violation of the no-service-surface shape defined here.
  - [[Standard - Information Architecture]] — A6 captures the commitment that the Triager does not traverse the service's IA at all; Triager-side aggregation is done with `aws` tooling against the S3 bucket, not through any endpoint on this service.
- Retrieval contract:
  - [[System - Debug Bundle Retention, Storage, and Key Scheme]] — the S3 key layout for the Debug Bundle flow determines how a Triager translates a Report ID from a URL into an S3 path for retrieval; the date-partitioned `<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<id>/bundle.tar.gz` key scheme is the only structural contract between this service and the Debug Bundle retrieval path.
  - [[System - Feedback Storage & Notification]] — the parallel System for the Feedback flow. Owns the Discord webhook push that is the Triager's **primary** surface for Feedback, the date-partitioned S3 key scheme under the `feedback/` prefix (`feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/feedback.json` + `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/feedback.txt` + optional `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/attachments/...`) used for the Triager's secondary S3-direct retrieval path, and the failure-self-reporting behavior that keeps Discord posts reliable.
  - [[System - Service Topology]] — the Triager's retrieval path is a separate IAM principal (the Fabro team member's own AWS credentials) orthogonal to the Fargate task role. A14 names the service-side half of S3 access and the Discord-webhook egress; the Triager half (S3 retrieval for both intake types; Discord reading) is outside the service topology entirely, which is how "never interacts with the intake service itself" holds up at the operational layer across both workflows.
- Accessibility:
  - [[Standard - Accessibility Policy]] — names the Triager explicitly as someone who does not use the service's surfaces, so no Triager-facing accessibility surface exists on the service; the only Triager-incidental surface is the delete button on the Report Metadata Page, which inherits the WCAG 2.2 AA + keyboard-first floor from that Standard.

## WHY: Rationale

- Thesis: [[Product Thesis - Pickup Point, Not Triage Surface]] — the Triager is half of what makes "pickup, not triage" a structurally coherent product. If Triagers had a surface here, this service would be a triage product with an intake front-door rather than a pickup point, and the thesis would collapse into marketing language over a different shape.
- Strategy: [[Principle - Strategy Support Effectiveness for Self-Hosted Users]] — every Triager-side design choice is checked against "does this help the team retrieve the Debug Bundle?" The current shape — direct S3 retrieval, no service surface — answers that question with the minimum possible product, which is what Strategy is buying.
- Scope: [[Principle - Narrow Forever]] — Triager-facing UI is the single most tempting drift target because "the team obviously needs to see all the Reports" is a seductive argument that disguises itself as common sense. The commitment framing of F6 — this is not deferred, not v2, not "we'll add a dashboard later" — is the upstream answer to that argument.
- Driver: Without this card, downstream product-layer cards risk inventing a Triager-facing surface by implication: a key scheme that assumes team listing, a retention policy that assumes team visibility, a metadata field that only makes sense on a dashboard. Naming the Triager once, with the no-service-surface shape load-bearing, lets every downstream card check itself against that shape before it drifts.

## HOW: Behavior

### Context: When a Triager Acts

A Triager is prompted into action by one of two external surfaces, depending on which intake type produced the signal. For Debug Bundles, the prompt is a `fabro.report` URL a Reporter pasted into a channel the Fabro team monitors — a GitHub issue filed against Fabro, a Slack message in a team or support channel, an email, or any other place Reporters are likely to paste it. For Feedback, the prompt is a Discord post in the private channel the service pushes Feedback notifications to via webhook. In neither case does the service itself deliver the prompt through a Triager-facing surface; both prompts arrive through external channels. The Triager's job is then to get from the prompt to the data (Debug Bundle or Feedback submission), act on it, and close out. Every step of both workflows happens outside this service.

### Workflow 1 — Debug Bundle Retrieval (primary, unchanged)

- Sees a `fabro.report` URL in a shared context (GitHub issue, Slack, email).
- Extracts the Report ID from the URL. (The Report ID is the load-bearing portion; possession of the URL is not required for retrieval once the ID is known — the Triager's access-control layer is AWS IAM, not URL possession.)
- Retrieves the Debug Bundle from S3 using their own AWS credentials, via the team's own AWS tooling (`aws s3 cp`, or whatever scripts / internal tools the team uses to work with the S3 key scheme).
- Works with the Debug Bundle in their own environment: untar it, inspect its contents, reproduce the bug, symbolicate anything that needs symbolication, render anything that needs rendering — all using Fabro's own tooling or general-purpose tools, never through `fabro.report`.
- Optionally, if the Reporter shared the URL with them and asks for the Report to be deleted, the Triager can visit the Report Metadata Page and use the delete button. This is the only way a Triager ever touches the service, and it is an incidental capability borrowed from URL possession — not a privileged Triager operation.

### Workflow 2 — Feedback Reading (new, parallel)

- Reads Feedback posts in the private Discord channel the service pushes Feedback notifications to via webhook. Discord is the **primary surface** — it carries the header (optional email), sysinfo one-liner (Fabro version / platform / arch), message body, attachments tree (if any), footer (Feedback ID, optional IP, S3 link), and optionally an attached tarball of files for smaller submissions. For most Feedback, reading the Discord post alone is sufficient to understand what the Feedback Submitter said.
- Optionally retrieves the full submission from S3 using their own AWS credentials, via the team's own AWS tooling (`aws s3 cp`, or whatever scripts / internal tools the team uses). This is **secondary** — used when the Discord post does not carry everything the Triager needs (e.g., an attachment that exceeded Discord's upload limit, or the tarball of attachments for a submission that did not attach inline). The S3 objects are at `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/feedback.json` (structured metadata) + `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/feedback.txt` (message text) + optional `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/attachments/...` (files). See [[System - Feedback Storage & Notification]] for the full key mapping.
- Acts on the Feedback out-of-band: files a GitHub issue against Fabro if the Feedback is a bug report worth tracking; adds the Feedback to the team's internal backlog if it is a feature request; thanks the Feedback Submitter out-of-band if the email is present and a response is warranted. **None of these actions involve `fabro.report` — the service's job ends at the Discord post.**
- Does **not** delete Feedback. The Feedback flow is fire-and-forget from the Feedback Submitter's side (no capability URL granting delete), and from the Triager's side there is no delete capability on this service for either flow's data. Feedback expires via the 30-day S3 lifecycle rule, same as Debug Bundles. See [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]].

### What a Triager Does Not Do

These apply across both workflows unless explicitly flow-specific:

- Does **not** log in to `fabro.report`. The service has no account concept for Triagers, for either flow.
- Does **not** visit a team dashboard. There is none, and there will not be one, for either flow. See [[Principle - Narrow Forever]].
- Does **not** browse a list of recent Reports or a list of recent Feedback on this service. No listing endpoint exists for either flow — Triagers discover Debug Bundle URLs through the out-of-band channels Reporters share them in, and discover Feedback through the Discord webhook push, not through the service.
- Does **not** filter Reports by Fabro version, metadata tag, or upload time through a service-side UI. Does **not** filter Feedback by submitter, topic, or timestamp through a service-side UI. Any aggregation, filtering, or searching the team wants to do is done either in Discord's own search (for Feedback) or with their own AWS tooling against the S3 bucket (for either flow), not on this service.
- Does **not** symbolicate, render, or inspect Debug Bundle contents through any endpoint of this service. Does **not** render, parse, classify, or aggregate Feedback contents through any endpoint of this service. Contents of either intake type are never exposed through `fabro.report`'s surfaces — the service writes to S3 and (for Feedback) pushes to Discord, and stops. See [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]].
- Does **not** receive notifications, alerts, or dashboards from this service about new Reports. Team awareness of Debug Bundles comes from the channels Reporters share URLs in, not from the service. Team awareness of Feedback comes from Discord, which the service posts to via webhook, but Discord is an external surface, not a service-hosted one.

### Examples

- **Example 1 — routine Debug Bundle retrieval.** A Reporter pastes a `fabro.report` URL into a GitHub issue titled "cargo install hangs on CI." A Triager sees the issue, copies the URL, extracts the Report ID from it, runs `aws s3 cp s3://fabro-report-bundles/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<id>/bundle.tar.gz ./bundle.tar.gz` (or the team's equivalent wrapper) using their own AWS credentials, untars the bundle, and begins investigation. The `fabro.report` service is not consulted at any step of the retrieval; it played no role after the Reporter's upload. See [[System - Debug Bundle Retention, Storage, and Key Scheme]] for the full key mapping.
- **Example 2 — Reporter asks for deletion after the fact.** A Reporter DMs a Triager a day after filing the issue and asks for the Report to be deleted because the Debug Bundle contained something sensitive. The Triager visits the URL, clicks the delete button, confirms the deletion, and is done. This interaction uses the Triager's URL possession as the authorization, not any Triager-specific privilege — any Reporter in possession of the URL could have done the same. See [[Decision - Capability URLs over Auth]].
- **Example 3 — routine Feedback reading (Discord-only).** A Feedback Submitter runs `fabro feedback "the new config loader is way faster, thanks" --email user@example.com`. The service assigns a UUIDv7, writes `feedback.json` + `feedback.txt` to S3 under the `feedback/` prefix, and posts a Discord message to the private channel with the email as the header, the sysinfo line, and the message body. A Triager reads the Discord post during the next team check-in, notes the praise, and does not take further action on this service. S3 is never consulted; Discord carried everything the Triager needed.
- **Example 4 — Feedback reading with S3 fallback.** A Feedback Submitter pipes a long log excerpt to `fabro feedback` along with two attached log files, one of which is 35MB. The service writes all three objects to S3 under `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/` and posts a Discord message with the message body, an attachments tree listing both files, and the S3 link in the footer. The 35MB file is too large to attach inline on Discord (graceful fallback: listed in the tree but not attached). A Triager reading the Discord post wants the full log file, so they follow the S3 link to locate the submission, then run `aws s3 cp s3://fabro-report-bundles/feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/attachments/large-log.txt ./` using their own AWS credentials. Discord was the primary surface; S3 was the secondary path; the service itself was not consulted at any retrieval step.

### Anti-Examples

- **Wrong:** A card describes a "Triager dashboard" that lists recent Reports or recent Feedback (or both). This is the single most common drift vector this card names and is permanently out-of-scope by [[Principle - Narrow Forever]] for both intake types. Any feature consumed by a Triager *through this service* violates the Triager role as defined. The correct shape is S3-direct retrieval for Debug Bundles, Discord-reading (with optional S3 retrieval) for Feedback — both external to the service.
- **Wrong:** An endpoint is added to `fabro.report` that accepts AWS credentials and returns a bundle or a feedback submission (a "convenience wrapper" over S3). This reintroduces a Triager-facing service surface the product explicitly refuses to have; it also duplicates AWS IAM with a second authorization system. See [[Decision - Capability URLs over Auth]] and [[Product Thesis - Pickup Point, Not Triage Surface]].
- **Wrong:** A card describes a Feedback-reading UI hosted by the service — a "Feedback inbox" page, a Feedback search endpoint, a Feedback-filter-by-topic feature on `fabro.report`. Discord is the primary Feedback surface precisely so the service has none. Moving Feedback reading from Discord onto a service-hosted page reintroduces the team-dashboard drift in a new shape and is the exact error this card exists to name.
- **Wrong:** A card or design describes the Triager's Feedback workflow as "the Triager filters Feedback in `fabro.report` before escalating." Filtering, classifying, aggregating, or searching Feedback through the service is out of scope. Triagers read Discord (which has its own search); the service writes and posts, and stops.
- **Wrong:** The Triager definition is widened to include "anyone with a support interest" — e.g., a third-party support partner, a hosted-Fabro team member (if that offering ever exists). This is an audience change under [[Decision - Who This Service Is For]], not an implementation change. Route it to the product owner; do not resolve it in a downstream card. The same applies to widening who reads the Feedback Discord channel beyond the Fabro team.
- **Wrong:** A card describes a Triager "log in" flow, a Triager account, or a Triager-specific permission layer on this service, for either flow. Triagers have no service-side identity on `fabro.report`; their identity is their AWS identity (for S3 retrieval) and their Discord channel membership (for Feedback reading), both of which live outside this service. See [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] and [[Decision - Capability URLs over Auth]].
- **Wrong:** A CLI string, a card, or a log line refers to a Triager as a "team member of fabro.report" or "maintainer." The Triager is a Fabro team member who retrieves bundles from S3 and reads Feedback in Discord; the phrase "fabro.report team member" implies a service-facing role that does not exist. See [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]].
- **Wrong:** A card conflates the two Triager workflows into one ("the Triager retrieves intake data from S3" as a single statement covering both flows). The two workflows differ in their primary surface — S3-direct for Debug Bundles, Discord for Feedback — and that asymmetry is load-bearing. Discord is genuinely different from S3-direct retrieval and should not be collapsed into it. See [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] Usage Rule 4 on the one-role-two-workflows shape.
