# Agent - Feedback Submitter

## WHAT: Definition

A **Feedback Submitter** is a self-hosted Fabro user who ran `fabro feedback` to send voice — a bug report in their own words, a feature request, praise, a complaint, or any general message — to the Fabro team. The Feedback Submitter's interaction with `fabro.report` is a single CLI invocation: they type (or pipe) some text, optionally attach files, optionally pass `-e, --email <email>`, press return, read one confirmation line on stdout, and close the terminal. That is the entire involvement. There is no URL, no rendered page, no account, no follow-up, no delete, no way to revisit the submission through the service, and no way for the service to reach the Feedback Submitter after the fact. The [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] entry for Feedback Submitter is the authoritative definition; this Agent card expands that entry with role context, emotional state, and anti-examples, but inherits the exact wording of the identity model and the canonical-name commitment.

**Feedback Submitter is not the same role as Reporter.** This is the single most load-bearing distinction on this card, because the library's default Agent is the Reporter, and an agent reading this card while primed by [[Agent - Reporter]] will be tempted to treat Feedback Submitter as "a Reporter who ran a different command." That reading is wrong and structurally misrepresents the product's two-intake-types-forever identity ([[Principle - Narrow Forever]]). The two roles differ in three ways that are substantive, not cosmetic:

- **Identity model is asymmetric.** A Reporter has *no identity on the service* — identified only by Fabro version metadata captured at upload time. A Feedback Submitter has an *optional email* (passed via `-e, --email` at CLI time, treated as an opaque string, never validated or used as an account token) *plus a server-logged client IP* (scoped strictly to abuse protection per [[Decision - IP Logging for Abuse Protection]]). Neither field creates an account, a login, a session, or a cross-submission identity; but both exist, and neither exists for the Reporter. See [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] Usage Rule 7 for the canonical statement of the asymmetry.
- **Post-pickup surface is asymmetric.** A Reporter receives a capability URL on upload and may optionally visit the Report Metadata Page (view ID, timestamp, size; click delete) until the 30-day retention deadline. A Feedback Submitter receives a CLI confirmation line and nothing else — no URL, no page, no delete action, no "my submissions" view. The Feedback Submitter's involvement ends at the moment the CLI prints the confirmation. This is not a missing feature; it is the fire-and-forget commitment recorded in [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]].
- **Emotional state is similar in shape but different in source.** The Reporter is efficient-and-reassured ([[Principle - Minimal Public Surface Feels Calm]]) because they are already frustrated by a Fabro bug and want to return to work. The Feedback Submitter is efficient-and-reassured because they are sharing voice with a team they trust and want confirmation that the message landed without ceremony — they are not (necessarily) in crisis state. Same register, different why.

A given human being may, at different moments, be both a Reporter (when they hit a bug and run `fabro dump --upload`) and a Feedback Submitter (when they run `fabro feedback` to share a feature request or general voice). The two roles share the same audience — self-hosted Fabro users per [[Decision - Who This Service Is For]] — and may be inhabited by the same person, but the *roles* are distinct and must not be conflated in card prose, CLI copy, Discord post wording, or code.

## WHERE: Ecosystem

- Identity and vocabulary:
  - [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] — the canonical **Feedback Submitter** definition (optional email + server-logged IP + Fabro version / platform / arch metadata; never "User," "Reporter," "Submitter" alone, or "Feedbacker") is the authoritative definition this card expands. Any drift between the two is a defect in one of them; the Standard is authoritative. Usage Rule 7 specifically forbids conflating Reporter and Feedback Submitter in prose or code.
- Counterpart role — asymmetric, not interchangeable:
  - [[Agent - Reporter]] — the persona for the Debug Bundle flow. The Reporter and the Feedback Submitter sit on the two intake types the product supports forever ([[Principle - Narrow Forever]]); their identity models and post-pickup surfaces are asymmetric by design. Reporter has no identity and a capability URL; Feedback Submitter has optional email + IP and nothing post-submission. See the "Reporter is not the same role as Feedback Submitter" note on that card for the counterpart framing.
- Team side of the pickup:
  - [[Agent - Triager]] — the Fabro team member who reads Feedback in Discord (primary surface) and optionally retrieves the full submission from S3 (secondary surface). The Feedback Submitter never interacts with the Triager through the service; the service's job ends at "Discord post sent." The Triager's Feedback workflow is the pickup side of the pickup, handled out-of-band exactly as the Triager's Debug Bundle workflow is.
- Audience commitment:
  - [[Decision - Who This Service Is For]] — the Feedback Submitter is a **self-hosted Fabro user**, primary and only. A hosted-Fabro offering's users are out of scope under that Decision; this card inherits the same audience shape as [[Agent - Reporter]]. Feedback Submitters and Reporters are drawn from the same audience; the audience commitment applies to the population, not to the role.
- Scope commitments:
  - [[Principle - Narrow Forever]] — the Feedback Submitter role exists because the product has exactly two intake types, forever (the 2026-04-23 ceiling). The role's narrow shape — one CLI, one confirmation, no post-submission surface — is the role-layer expression of that ceiling. A third persona card representing a third intake type cannot arrive without a product-owner identity-change conversation.
  - [[Product Thesis - Pickup Point, Not Triage Surface]] — the Feedback Submitter is the drop-off endpoint of the Feedback pickup. They drop the message at the pickup point and leave; Discord is where the Fabro team picks it up. The service hosts no triage surface on either side.
  - [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]] — post-submission Feedback-Submitter-facing enrichment (a "my submissions" view, a delete link in the Discord post, a feedback-analytics dashboard for the submitter, a capability URL granting access to the submission) is a drift shape on the Feedback side, parallel in shape to the post-upload Reporter-facing enrichment F3 rejects on the Debug Bundle side. Adding any of those widens the Feedback Submitter's post-pickup surface beyond the fire-and-forget commitment.
- Access model — asymmetric with Reporter:
  - [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]] — the inverse-shape Decision for the Feedback flow. Where [[Decision - Capability URLs over Auth]] gives Reporters a capability URL that grants view + delete, A19 gives Feedback Submitters nothing post-submission: no URL, no page, no delete. The two Decisions are paired; together they express the two-intake-types-forever identity at the access-model layer.
  - [[Decision - Capability URLs over Auth]] — explicitly scoped to the Debug Bundle flow. The Feedback Submitter does *not* inherit that Decision; they inherit A19 instead.
- Identity-data scope:
  - [[Decision - IP Logging for Abuse Protection]] — the scope commitment that binds IP logging to abuse protection only. The Feedback Submitter's IP is logged in `feedback.json` (per [[Component - Feedback]]) and retained under the 30-day standard, but is never used for analytics, cross-submission tracking, marketing telemetry, compliance audit trail, or geolocation surfacing. This is the library's first identity-data capture; the Decision is the guardrail that keeps it scoped.
- Tone of the interaction:
  - [[Principle - Minimal Public Surface Feels Calm]] — the emotional goal for the Feedback Submitter is **efficient and reassured**. The CLI confirmation line is compact, confirms that the submission landed, and does not celebrate ("Thanks!"), apologize ("Sorry to bother you"), or promise a response the service cannot make ("We'll get back to you soon"). Same register as the Reporter's success output, different source emotion (sharing voice with a trusted team, not crisis-state handoff).
- Strategic why:
  - [[Principle - Strategy Support Effectiveness for Self-Hosted Users]] — the Feedback Submitter carries the **voice** mechanism of the Strategy's two-mechanism shape. Debug Bundles carry reproducibility data (the Reporter's mechanism); Feedback carries voice (the Feedback Submitter's mechanism). Both mechanisms serve the same Strategic WHY — *make it easy for the team to help self-hosted Fabro users* — by giving the team complementary signal. The three Strategy anti-goals (no formal support SLA, no compliance posture, no marketing telemetry) apply equally to the Feedback Submitter's data; none of those are loosened just because the Feedback flow captures identity-adjacent data (email + IP).
- Referenced by:
  - [[Component - Feedback]] — the submission event the Feedback Submitter creates when they run `fabro feedback`; carries the authoritative field list the Feedback Submitter contributes to (UUIDv7 ID, message text, file attachments manifest, optional email, server-logged IP, Fabro version / platform / arch, timestamp, retention deadline).
  - [[System - Feedback Storage & Notification]] — the operational mechanism the Feedback Submitter's request traverses: the CLI POSTs to `/v1/feedback` on the Fargate service; the service writes S3 objects under the `feedback/` prefix and pushes a notification to the team's Discord channel via webhook. A17 owns the mechanism; the Feedback Submitter does not see any of it beyond the CLI confirmation.
  - [[Standard - Interaction Patterns (CLI + Delete)]] — the `fabro feedback` CLI UX specifics (positional text / files, `-e, --email` flag, stdin acceptance, spinner, confirmation shape, no-URL-returned, no-delete commitment) the Feedback Submitter actually experiences.
  - [[Standard - Accessibility Policy]] — `fabro feedback` is part of the `NO_COLOR`-aware CLI set; a Feedback Submitter operating the terminal without color, with a screen reader, or keyboard-only must still reach the fire-and-forget flow cleanly.
  - [[Standard - Information Architecture]] — A6's IA shape is two parallel minimal IAs; the Feedback Submitter's IA traversal is exactly "run `fabro feedback`, read the confirmation line, close the terminal." No browse, no navigate, no service-hosted page.
  - [[Standard - Competitive Context]] — `bun feedback` is named there as the CLI-intake template for `fabro feedback`; the Feedback Submitter's CLI shape is the self-hosted-Fabro-user adaptation of that template.

## WHY: Rationale

- Strategy: [[Principle - Strategy Support Effectiveness for Self-Hosted Users]] — the Feedback Submitter role exists because *voice* is one of the two data shapes the Fabro team needs to help self-hosted users. A Debug Bundle without voice tells the team what broke; voice without reproducibility data tells the team what the user wanted; the two together let the team decide whether to file a bug, pick up a feature request, or respond to praise. The Feedback Submitter's narrow shape — one CLI, one confirmation, no post-submission surface — is calibrated so the friction on *delivering voice* is as low as possible.
- Thesis: [[Product Thesis - Pickup Point, Not Triage Surface]] — the Feedback Submitter is one endpoint of the Feedback pickup. They drop the message and leave; Discord is where the Fabro team picks it up; the service plays no role after "post sent." A Feedback Submitter who stayed on the service after submission to read their own message would be evidence the product had drifted toward a feedback-forum shape, which the thesis refuses for the same reason it refuses a triage surface on the Debug Bundle flow.
- Scope: [[Principle - Narrow Forever]] — the Feedback Submitter role is a direct expression of the 2026-04-23 absolute ceiling at two intake types. Before 2026-04-23 the library had one persona card for the intake side of the pickup ([[Agent - Reporter]]); after 2026-04-23 it has two, and will have exactly two forever. A third persona card representing a third intake type requires a product-owner identity-change conversation, not an implementation decision here.
- Driver: Without this card, downstream product-layer cards (Component - Feedback, System - Feedback Storage & Notification, Standard - Interaction Patterns, Standard - Accessibility Policy) each risk inventing their own implicit Feedback Submitter — one that has an account, one that can delete, one that has a "my submissions" view. Naming the Feedback Submitter once, with the asymmetric identity model and the fire-and-forget surface load-bearing, prevents each downstream card from silently widening the role. This is the same role-layer driver that [[Agent - Reporter]] carries for the Debug Bundle flow.

## HOW: Behavior

### Context: When a Feedback Submitter Arrives

A Feedback Submitter is a self-hosted Fabro user who decided to share something with the Fabro team — a bug report in their own words (not a Debug Bundle; just their description of what went wrong), a feature request, praise for a recent release, a complaint, a documentation gap they noticed, or any other voice. Crucially, a Feedback Submitter is **not necessarily in crisis state**. They may be:

- A satisfied user who typed `fabro feedback "love the new config loader, thanks"` after finishing a task.
- A user who hit a Fabro bug but did not run `fabro dump --upload` because they wanted to describe the bug in their own words first before (or instead of) shipping a Debug Bundle.
- A user who has an idea for a feature and wants the Fabro team to see it.
- A user who has a minor complaint or a piece of documentation feedback and wants to pass it along.
- A user who is forwarding someone else's feedback on behalf of their team (the `-e, --email` flag optionally attributes the message).

The emotional shape is: *I have something to say; let me say it; let me know it landed; let me close the terminal.* The Feedback Submitter's time is respected by the CLI's confirmation shape ([[Principle - Minimal Public Surface Feels Calm]]'s "efficient and reassured" register), not by a promise of a response, a thank-you animation, or a follow-up flow. Warmth that slows the handoff is worse than no warmth at all.

### What the Feedback Submitter Does

- Runs `fabro feedback` with one of the invocation shapes confirmed in [[Standard - Interaction Patterns (CLI + Delete)]]:
  - `fabro feedback "the new config loader is way faster, thanks"` — positional text, no files, no email.
  - `fabro feedback report.txt details.log` — positional file attachments, no text.
  - `fabro feedback "please document the flag ordering rule for --tag"` — positional text covering a feature request or bug-in-words.
  - `echo "please document X" | fabro feedback --email user@example.com` — stdin text + optional email attribution.
- Reads the CLI's confirmation line — compact, in the `bun`-calibrated register per [[Principle - Minimal Public Surface Feels Calm]]. Closes the terminal. The involvement is over.

That is the entire list. There is nothing else the Feedback Submitter does on or around the service.

### What the Feedback Submitter Does Not Do

- Does **not** log in. There is no account concept on this service for Feedback Submitters, for the same reason there is no account concept for Reporters: per [[Principle - Narrow Forever]], no auth, no accounts, no per-user scoping. The optional `-e, --email` is not an account token; it is an opaque attribution string.
- Does **not** receive a URL. No Report Metadata Page exists for the Feedback flow; there is nothing a URL could point at. See [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]].
- Does **not** visit a page. The service renders no page for the Feedback flow — not a receipt page, not a "thanks" page, not a "my submissions" page. There is no service-hosted surface the Feedback Submitter could visit after submission.
- Does **not** delete. There is no recall mechanism — no CLI subcommand (`fabro feedback delete <id>`), no link in the Discord post, no service endpoint (`DELETE /v1/feedback/<id>`). A Feedback Submitter who regrets a submission must contact the Fabro team out-of-band; the team can manually delete the S3 objects if the request is legitimate, but this happens outside the service. See [[Standard - Interaction Patterns (CLI + Delete)]] "No Delete on the Feedback Flow" and [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]].
- Does **not** see a "my feedback" view, a list of past submissions, or any cross-submission history. The service does not have Feedback Submitter identity to tie past submissions to, by design.
- Does **not** edit a submission after the fact. Submissions are immutable from the moment the CLI prints the confirmation; if a follow-up thought occurs, the Feedback Submitter runs `fabro feedback` again with a new message.
- Does **not** interact with Triagers through the service. Communication between a Feedback Submitter and the Fabro team, if it happens, happens out-of-band — via the optional email the Submitter provided, or via channels separate from `fabro.report` entirely.
- Does **not** receive notifications from the service. No "your feedback was read," no "the team responded," no "your submission expired." The service's job ends at the Discord post.
- Does **not** have access to the S3 objects for their submission. The S3 bucket is private; only the Fabro team's own AWS credentials read from it ([[Agent - Triager]] Feedback workflow, secondary surface). The Feedback Submitter never touches S3 directly.

### Examples

- **Example 1 — the typical path (praise).** A self-hosted Fabro user finishes a task and decides to send a note to the team. They run `fabro feedback "the new config loader is way faster, thanks" --email user@example.com`. The CLI POSTs the payload to `/v1/feedback` on the Fargate service, prints a short confirmation line ("Feedback sent to the Fabro team." or similar, exact wording per [[Standard - Interaction Patterns (CLI + Delete)]]), and exits. The Feedback Submitter closes the terminal. The service, meanwhile, writes `feedback.json` + `feedback.txt` to S3 under `feedback/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<uuidv7>/`, posts a Discord message with the email as the header, the sysinfo one-liner, and the message body. A Triager reads the Discord post during the next team check-in, notes the praise, and takes no further action on the service. The Feedback Submitter's involvement ended when the CLI exited.
- **Example 2 — the feature-request path with an attachment.** A Feedback Submitter wants the `--tag` flag to support reading from a file. They pipe a short description from stdin and attach a draft config they've been experimenting with: `cat draft-config.txt | fabro feedback --email user@example.com`. The CLI treats the piped text as the message body, attaches `draft-config.txt` (if it were also passed positionally; in this example only stdin is piped, so no attachment), and POSTs to the service. The CLI prints confirmation. The service writes the objects, posts to Discord. The Feedback Submitter closes the terminal and goes back to work. If the Fabro team wants to follow up on the feature request, they use the email address from the Discord header — not the service, which has no follow-up affordance.
- **Example 3 — the Feedback Submitter regrets the submission.** A Feedback Submitter types `fabro feedback "this is a rant about X"` in frustration, reads the confirmation line, and immediately regrets the tone. There is no delete action on the service. The Feedback Submitter cannot recall the submission from the CLI, cannot visit a page to delete, cannot click a link in the Discord post (there is no such link). Their correct recourse is to contact the Fabro team out-of-band — using the email they optionally attached, using Discord directly if they are a team channel member, or via another channel entirely — and ask the team to delete the S3 objects manually. The service itself offers no recall; this is the deliberate fire-and-forget shape per [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]].

### Anti-Examples

- **Wrong:** Designing any feature that presumes the Feedback Submitter will return to the service after submission to *read* their submission, *delete* it, *edit* it, or *view* its status. The Feedback Submitter's post-submission surface is nothing. Any feature that restores a post-submission surface — a "view my feedback" endpoint, a "delete my feedback" CLI subcommand, a capability URL returned on success, a link in the Discord post — is a drift into the shape [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]] refuses. The correct answer to "the Feedback Submitter wants to see what they sent" is: they can re-read their terminal history or their text editor; the service is not the memory of their submission.
- **Wrong:** Treating the Feedback Submitter as "the user" in the generic sense implied by most web products. This product has no "user" concept — only Reporters (no identity), Feedback Submitters (optional email + logged IP, scoped strictly per [[Decision - IP Logging for Abuse Protection]]), and Triagers (own AWS credentials, Discord channel membership). See [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] Usage Rules 4 and 7. A card, CLI string, or Discord-post template that refers to a Feedback Submitter as "the user" has already started to drift toward a generic web-product identity model this product rejects.
- **Wrong:** Calling a Feedback Submitter a "Reporter." The two roles are asymmetric in their identity models (optional email + logged IP vs. no identity) and their post-pickup surfaces (nothing vs. capability URL) — see [[Standard - Noun Vocabulary]] Usage Rule 7. A CLI string that says "Reporter" in `fabro feedback`'s output misrepresents the role and erodes the canonical-vocabulary commitment that keeps the two intakes distinct. The correct noun is "Feedback Submitter," exactly.
- **Wrong:** Treating the optional email as an account-shaped field. The email is an opaque attribution string — not validated for format at the Standard layer, not tied to a login, not used to authorize any subsequent action, not used to group submissions into a "my feedback" view, not used as a notification target for service-initiated messages. An implementation or card that treats the email as an account key has widened the role beyond what this card and [[Decision - IP Logging for Abuse Protection]] permit.
- **Wrong:** Treating the server-logged IP as anything other than an abuse-protection signal. The IP is scoped strictly by [[Decision - IP Logging for Abuse Protection]] — not analytics, not cross-submission tracking, not marketing telemetry, not compliance audit trail, not geolocation surfacing. A feature that surfaces the IP anywhere except inside `feedback.json` for Triager inspection during abuse investigation, or that cross-references IPs across submissions for "same user" detection, is a scope expansion this card and A18 together refuse.
- **Wrong:** Adding a post-submission follow-up flow — a "how was your feedback experience?" prompt, a "we received your submission, we'll get back to you within 48 hours" promise, a "your feedback has been escalated to the team" status update. The Feedback Submitter's involvement ends at the CLI confirmation; anything that implies ongoing contact from the service is violating the fire-and-forget commitment and re-introducing the exact post-pickup surface the Feedback flow was designed not to have. See [[Decision - Fire-and-Forget Feedback]] and [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]].
