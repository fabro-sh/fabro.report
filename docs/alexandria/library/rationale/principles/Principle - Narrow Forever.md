# Principle - Narrow Forever (Roadmap as Commitment)

## WHAT: The Principle

The scope of `fabro.report` is **exactly two intake types — Debug Bundles and Feedback — forever.** This is a permanent identity decision, not a v1 simplification. The Roadmap for this product is not a plan that lists future features; it is a commitment that names future features — and future *kinds* of features — that will never be built. "Narrow forever" is the load-bearing framing — first confirmed on 2026-04-22 by the product owner as a one-intake identity, broadened on 2026-04-23 by the same product owner to a **two-intake identity with an absolute ceiling at two** — and it means exactly what it says: assume forever.

**No third intake type, forever.** A third kind of submission surface — not an extension to Debug Bundles, not an extension to Feedback, but a *new kind* of pickup shape — is explicitly out of scope at the identity level. Adding a third intake type is a Strategy change, not an implementation decision. It requires a product-owner identity-change conversation before any design or implementation work begins. This ceiling is new on 2026-04-23 and is the shape "narrow forever" takes after the scope broadened from one intake to two. "Narrow forever" now operates at two levels simultaneously: **narrow within each intake type** (no symbolication, no rendering, no dashboards, no triage UI on either flow) **and narrow at the set of intake types** (no third kind of submission, ever).

Scope expansion *within* either intake toward a fuller `bun.report`-shaped product (symbolication, in-browser rendering of Debug Bundle contents, markdown rendering, feedback collection as a post-upload extension to the Debug Bundle flow, team dashboards, triage UIs, per-user accounts, cross-upload identity tracking) is permanently out-of-scope, not "future work." That is the feature-layer identity decision. A Roadmap on this product, read correctly, reads as a list of rejections — and the list applies across **both** intake types equally.

This Principle exists because "narrow" is a word that decays. Every software product that has ever been small has been told, by someone acting in good faith, that it should grow. The decay path is consistent: first the scope is "narrow for v1"; then it is "narrow for now"; then it is "narrow, but we'll revisit"; then a feature lands that would have been rejected at "narrow for v1" and the narrowness commitment is already gone. `fabro.report` short-circuits that decay by recording, upstream and in writing, that the scope is not "narrow for v1" — it is *narrow forever*. That phrasing makes scope expansion (both *within* each intake and *at the set of intake types*) a Strategy change requiring product-owner approval, not an implementation decision to be argued feature-by-feature.

The 2026-04-23 broadening from one intake to two was itself a Strategy-change conversation conducted with the product owner, not an implementation decision. It followed the process this Principle exists to require. The ceiling is now locked at two; any future broadening must follow the same process.

## WHY: Belief

Products drift because "scope for v1" is defaulted to "scope we can defend only for v1." An agent acting in good faith will, correctly, treat v1-shaped constraints as relaxable when v1 ships. `fabro.report` is a greenfield product, which means v1 has not yet shipped, which means every agent touching this library is in a position to treat the narrow scope as v1-shaped. That is the drift risk this Principle addresses.

The commitment is phrased as identity rather than as strategy because identity changes require a different kind of approval than strategy changes. A strategy change is a trade-off re-evaluation: "we used to think X; we now think Y." An identity change is a product re-founding: "this product is not what it was." The scope of `fabro.report` is an identity commitment precisely so that any expansion proposal lands on the product owner's desk as a re-founding question, not as a strategy question, and has to clear a high bar before anything ships.

The cost of this Principle is that it forecloses futures. `fabro.report` will not, under this Principle, ever grow into a triage product, a crash-analysis tool, a team dashboard, or a telemetry feed — even if those would be valuable products. That cost is accepted because the alternative — a product that carries the narrowness like a v1 constraint — costs the commitment entirely. A half-committed narrowness is indistinguishable from gradual expansion, and gradual expansion is how `fabro.report` would become a second-rate `bun.report` clone. Narrow forever is the only shape of this commitment that survives contact with agents.

## HOW: Application

### Permanently Out-of-Scope List (The Roadmap-as-Rejection)

The following features exist in adjacent products (notably `bun.report`). Under this Principle, none of them will be built in `fabro.report`, now or ever. This is the complete list of the major drift shapes known at initialize time; the list can grow as new drift shapes are noticed, but items do not leave the list. **Every item below applies across both intake types equally** — Debug Bundles *and* Feedback. No item becomes allowed on the Feedback flow just because the Feedback flow is newer; no item becomes allowed on the Debug Bundle flow just because it predates Feedback. The rejection list is intake-type-agnostic.

- **Symbolication (of either flow).** No server-side crash parser, stack-frame resolver, or symbolication service. Symbolication of a Debug Bundle's contents belongs to Triagers with their own tooling, retrieving from S3 directly. Any equivalent server-side parsing of Feedback text or attachments to extract structured crash data is the same drift in a different intake and is equally out of scope.
- **Sentry integration as a bundle or feedback processor (and any crash-reporting SaaS integration of that shape).** No third-party integrations that expect structured crash payloads to be forwarded by the service. Debug Bundles and Feedback attachments are opaque to this service. (Service-side operational Sentry — tracking the service's own errors — is a separate, allowed use; see [[Decision - Sentry for Service Errors, Not Bundle Processing]] and the inline distinction in [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]].)
- **Debug Bundle content rendering.** No in-browser view of files inside the tarball. Not on the Report Metadata Page, not at a secondary endpoint, not via a "convenience" render.
- **Markdown rendering of structured crash data (across either flow).** Related to but distinct from bundle rendering; `bun.report` renders stack frames and logs as formatted markdown. `fabro.report` renders nothing from inside Debug Bundles, and renders nothing from inside Feedback beyond what the Discord webhook's component-v2 payload (see forward-reference to `System - Feedback Storage & Notification`, manifest A17) structurally requires.
- **Feedback collection as a post-bundle-followup.** No post-upload feedback form, follow-up email, or "tell us what happened" flow on the Report Metadata Page. The optional reporter message at CLI upload time is the entire Reporter-side feedback surface on the Debug Bundle flow. (This is **not** a rejection of the standalone `fabro feedback` CLI — see [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]]'s Sentry-distinction-pattern note for the boundary. `fabro feedback` is a separate intake type with its own persona, entity, and system cards; it is not a bundle-flow extension.)
- **Team dashboards (for either flow).** No team-facing surface listing recent Reports, filtering by Fabro version, aggregating Report data, listing recent Feedback submissions, aggregating feedback themes, or providing any service-hosted browsing of either data type. Triagers retrieve Debug Bundles directly from S3 with their own credentials; the Fabro team reads Feedback from Discord (with optional S3 retrieval). That is the entire team-side interface for both intakes. A service-hosted Feedback browsing surface is as out of scope as a service-hosted Report dashboard, and for the same reason.
- **Triage UIs (on either flow).** No Triager-facing screens of any kind on the intake service, for either intake type. The Fabro team does not interact with the service itself to triage Debug Bundles or Feedback. Triage behavior happens in S3 (for bundles), in Discord (for feedback — where the Discord post is a push-consumed surface, not a service-rendered one), in GitHub issues, and in Fabro's own internal tooling.
- **Per-user accounts (across either flow).** No account concept, no login, no user record. Reporters have no identity on the service by design. Feedback Submitters have at most an optional `--email` and a server-logged IP — neither of which creates an account, a login, or a cross-submission user record. Adding any of those to either flow is scope expansion under this Principle.
- **Cross-submission identity tracking (across either flow).** No "this looks like the same user as last week" reasoning across Debug Bundle uploads, across Feedback submissions, or across one intake type linked to another. Reporters and Feedback Submitters have no identity across submissions by design. IP logging on the Feedback flow is scoped strictly to abuse protection (see forward-reference to `Decision - IP Logging for Abuse Protection`, manifest A18) — not cross-submission tracking, not attribution analytics, not cross-intake-type joining.

Each of these items is something an agent could be asked to build "since you're already there." Under this Principle, the answer is no, and the answer does not need to be re-derived each time — this Principle is the derivation. The 2026-04-23 scope expansion did not remove any item from this list; it retrofitted the framing so each item is explicitly scoped across both intake types.

### No Third Intake Type, Forever (Identity-Level Ceiling)

The list above enumerates drift shapes *within* the two current intake types. Beyond that, this Principle also rejects **any third intake type** at the identity layer. The 2026-04-23 scope expansion ceiling is absolute at two: Debug Bundles and Feedback, exactly, forever. Examples of proposals that would constitute a third intake type and are therefore ruled out at the identity layer:

- A "log upload" intake — uploading arbitrary log files as first-class submissions, distinct from Debug Bundles and Feedback.
- A "screenshot upload" intake — uploading screenshots as a distinct submission type, whether or not it resembles a feedback attachment.
- A "telemetry event" intake — streaming or batched anonymous-usage telemetry, distinct from the voice-carrying Feedback intake.
- A "support ticket" intake — anything that introduces a ticket-shaped entity with state transitions, distinct from the fire-and-forget Feedback shape.
- A "session recording" intake — uploading session recordings, whether or not they are rendered.

Each of the above is adjacent to the existing two intakes and could arrive innocently as "we already accept X, let's accept Y too." Under this Principle, any such proposal is a product re-founding conversation with the product owner, not an implementation decision. The list above is illustrative; the commitment applies to any new *kind* of submission surface, regardless of whether the specific shape appears in the examples.

### Commitment vs Plan Framing

A Roadmap is usually read as "features planned for the future." This product's Roadmap — this Principle — is read as "features named for the future in order to commit to not building them." That inversion matters.

- The commitment framing means a design discussion that starts "eventually we'll need X" where X is on the out-of-scope list above should be answered "no, we permanently will not need X in this product." Not "that's future work." Not "someday." Not "interesting — let's revisit." The agent should stop that branch of the conversation.
- The commitment framing means adding a feature to the "not now, but maybe later" pile is itself a violation. There is no "maybe later" pile for the out-of-scope list. If an item moves off the list, it is because the product owner explicitly reopened scope as a Strategy change, not because an agent quietly relabeled it.
- The commitment framing means this Principle can be cited against proposals at any stage: design, implementation, review, or retrospective. "We said narrow forever" is a complete answer to a scope-expansion proposal.

### Pairing with F3 (Concrete Drift List) and F5 (Strategy)

This Principle pairs deliberately with [[Principle - Reject Like bun.report Drift]] and [[Principle - Strategy Support Effectiveness for Self-Hosted Users]] to form a three-card enforcement frame:

- F5 (Strategy) says *what the product is optimizing for* — team retrieval of Debug Bundles from self-hosted Fabro users, with explicit anti-goals.
- This Principle (F6, Narrow Forever) says *the scope that serves that Strategy is permanent*. Scope is not a v1 constraint subject to relaxation.
- F3 (Reject Like bun.report Drift) says *here are the specific feature shapes that would violate the commitment*, with concrete drift examples.

An agent facing a scope-expansion proposal should be able to cite the full chain: Strategy rules out the direction of the expansion; Narrow Forever rules out the timing argument ("maybe later"); Reject Like bun.report Drift rules out the specific feature shape. All three are required, because each closes a different escape hatch.

### Decision Heuristic

When someone proposes a feature that expands scope toward the out-of-scope list — or toward a new shape not yet on the list but structurally similar — ask four questions in order:

1. **Does the proposal constitute a third intake type** (a new *kind* of submission surface, neither a Debug Bundle nor a Feedback)? If yes, this is an identity-layer scope change and must be routed to the product owner as an identity-change conversation. Do not design; do not prototype; do not treat as implementation.
2. **Is the feature on the permanently-out-of-scope list above, or a near-shape of something on that list, within either existing intake type?** If yes, reject and cite this Principle. Do not implement; do not defer; do not queue for later.
3. **If not on the list, would accepting this feature move the product toward triage-surface shape rather than pickup-point shape — on either intake?** Check [[Product Thesis - Pickup Point, Not Triage Surface]]. If yes, this is a drift shape that should be named and added to the list, and rejected.
4. **Does the proposal explicitly flag itself as a scope change requiring product-owner approval?** If yes, route to the product owner as a Strategy change, not as an implementation decision. If no, the proposal is a drift and should be reframed or rejected.

The identity decision is named "narrow forever" because that is what it is. Agents reading this card should treat that phrasing — along with "intake-only" (now read as "intake-only within each of the two intake types"), "pickup point," "permanently out-of-scope," and the new "two intake types, forever" / "no third intake type, forever" commitments — as load-bearing. Do not soften to "intentionally narrow," "narrow for this release," "narrow for the foreseeable future," "two intakes for now," or "two intakes by default." The commitments are forever. That is the shape of the product.
